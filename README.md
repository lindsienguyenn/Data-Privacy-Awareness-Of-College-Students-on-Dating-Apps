## Overview

This research investigates the "Technical Overconfidence" paradox in cybersecurity by examining how technical knowledge, loneliness, and social support influence scam susceptibility among Computer Science (CS) and non-Computer Science students.

Despite reporting higher confidence in their security expertise, CS students demonstrate greater willingness to engage in risky dating app behaviors—including clicking verification links and moving conversations to private channels. This study explores the critical gap between perceived safety and actual behavioral vulnerability when students face emotional social engineering attacks.

**Key Findings:**
- Technical expertise does not eliminate vulnerability; CS students showed greater risky behavior despite higher cybersecurity knowledge
- Emotional and social factors contribute significantly to scam susceptibility
- Prevention strategies must combine technical training with emotional awareness and social engineering education

**Future Work:**
- Test intervention effectiveness through brief training on emotional awareness and digital safety
- Improve scenario realism to better capture authentic emotional responses in hypothetical situations
